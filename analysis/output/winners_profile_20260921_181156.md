# Winners Profile — 20260921_181156

ANALYSIS-ONLY. Read-only, no trading logic touched. Built on top of `edge_research_dataset_20260921_171634.csv`.

## Method

- Started from 551 candidates in the edge-research dataset (shadow trades + OBSERVING trajectories).
- Filtered to 485 with a real price path (n_observations_used_full >= 5) and no outage overlap.
- "Did well" = top quartile of `forward_return_full` (realized return from decision-time price to the LAST available snapshot — not peak return, so a spike-then-rug does not count as a winner just because it touched a high peak).
- Quartile threshold: forward_return_full >= 22.1%. Winners: n=122. Rest: n=363.
- "Straight movement" = `path_efficiency` (net move / sum of absolute step-to-step moves, computed from the token's actual snapshot price path). Near +1 = moved steadily in one direction; near 0 = round-tripped a lot to end up where it started.

## ADDENDUM: winner rate by group and gate — the single biggest finding here

Computed after the initial run, in response to a direct follow-up question. This is the
most important number in this whole report.

| segment | n | winners | winner rate | median forward_return_full |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **whole pool (baseline)** | 485 | 122 | 25.2% | — |
| OBSERVING (Tier1/Scorer rejects, control group) | 347 | 113 | **32.6%** | **+7.1%** |
| SHADOW (scorer_v2_threshold_4, score>=4.0 crossers) | 138 | 9 | **6.5%** | **-91.5%** |
| — within OBSERVING, gated at TIER1 specifically | 339 | 112 | 33.0% | — |
| — within TIER1 rejects, reason=LP_NOT_BURNED | 129 | 48 | 37.2% | — |
| — within TIER1 rejects, reason=WASH_TRADING | 210 | 64 | 30.5% | — |

Tokens the bot outright REJECTS at Tier1 (before they're ever scored — median age at
rejection is 0.5-0.6 minutes, i.e. Tier1 runs almost immediately at discovery) end up in the
winners bucket at roughly 1.3x the baseline rate, with a positive median outcome. Tokens the
bot's OWN scorer flags as promising enough to cross the shadow-experiment's score>=4.0
threshold end up in the winners bucket at barely a quarter of the baseline rate, with a median
outcome of losing over 90% of value by the end of the tracked window.

The WASH_TRADING vs LP_NOT_BURNED split above is NOT distinctive — those are the only two
Tier1 rejection reasons that occur anywhere in this dataset (388 WASH_TRADING / 337
LP_NOT_BURNED out of 725 total Tier1 rejects, i.e. every single Tier1 reject is one or the
other), so the winners' composition by reason just mirrors the population's composition. LP_NOT_BURNED rejects do have a somewhat higher winner rate than WASH_TRADING rejects
(37.2% vs 30.5%) but n is not huge on either side and this was not time-split validated —
treat as a minor secondary lead, not the headline.

**The most likely explanation, and why this is not simply "the scorer is broken":**
Shadow trades can only trigger AFTER the Scorer has enough rolling-window history to compute
a score — several snapshots into a token's life, by construction later than a Tier1 reject
(which fires in the first ~30-60 seconds). `edge_research.py` already found `age_minutes` at
decision time negatively correlated with 30m peak return (rho=-0.25, p=0.0035) for the shadow
population specifically. So a real, and less alarming, explanation is: by the time a token's
score crosses 4.0, a meaningful part of its move has often already happened BEFORE that
decision point, and "forward_return_full" (measured only from the decision point onward) is
structurally biased to look worse for a later decision-time than an earlier one, independent
of whether the Scorer's judgment about the token's quality is right or wrong. This does not
fully explain a -91.5% MEDIAN outcome, though — that is a severe enough result that it
deserves direct investigation (e.g. re-running this comparing TOTAL lifetime return from
discovery, not just forward-from-decision return, for the shadow population) before drawing a
firm conclusion about the scorer itself. Flagging this explicitly as the top follow-up
question rather than answering it here, since the user asked for research, not a rule change.
